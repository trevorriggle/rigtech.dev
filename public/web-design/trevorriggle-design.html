One question to answer

A hiring manager opening a designer's portfolio wants to know one thing: can this person do the job? For a designer who also builds, the answer has to cover both, without turning into two sites.

So the headline is the job title, and everything under it is work that shipped.

What's on it

Applications: DrawEvolve, thoosie and Lynk. Design: American Scientific, Taranto's, and personal work. Agentic AI: four agents in production, starting with the billing agent.

Every case study opens the same way, with context, stack and links at the side, so they can be compared at a glance.

The costs are on it too

Most portfolios only show what worked. This one includes an architecture that had to be migrated, features that were cut, and Lynk, a product shelved because the economics didn't work.

That's the part that tells someone how decisions get made.
